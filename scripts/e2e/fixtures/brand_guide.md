# Brand voice guide

This guide describes how our listings should sound on every marketplace. It is written for the
people who review copy before it goes live, and for the tools that draft that copy. Read it once
in full, then keep the glossary close while you work. When two rules seem to pull in different
directions, the rule about honesty wins over the rule about style.

## Who we are

We make everyday ethnic wear and home goods for families who care about fabric, fit and finish.
Our customers shop on their phones, often late in the evening, and they compare many listings
side by side before they buy. They are careful with money and they notice when a listing promises
more than the product can give. Our voice should feel like a helpful shopkeeper who knows the
stock well: warm, direct and specific, never pushy and never vague.

## Tone

- Write in plain, friendly language that a first-time buyer can follow without a dictionary.
- Lead with what the customer can see and touch: the fabric, the weave, the colour and the fit.
- Prefer short sentences. One idea per sentence reads well on a small screen.
- Use the active voice. Say "the kurta has side pockets" rather than "side pockets are provided".
- Keep the mood calm and confident. Excitement belongs in the photos, not in the punctuation.
- Avoid exclamation marks in titles and bullets. One in a long description is the most we allow.
- Do not use slang, memes or trending phrases; they age quickly and they read poorly in translation.
- Address the reader as "you" when it helps, but never assume their gender, age or occasion.

## Claims and honesty

Every claim in a listing must be one we can stand behind if a customer or a marketplace asks.
We describe the product as it is, and we let the customer decide whether it suits them.

- Never call a product the best, the finest or number one, and never say it is guaranteed.
- Do not promise delivery dates, discounts or stock levels in the copy; the marketplace shows those.
- State the fabric exactly as the label does. If a fabric is a blend, name every fibre in it.
- Describe colours as they look in daylight, and mention when a dye may vary between batches.
- Give care instructions that match the label: hand wash, gentle machine wash or dry clean only.
- Do not mention competitors, other brands or other marketplaces by name.
- Avoid health, safety or environmental claims unless a certificate supports them, and name it.
- When a size runs small or large, say so plainly and point the reader to the size chart.

## Titles

A title is the first thing a shopper reads, and often the only thing. It should name the product
clearly and help the shopper tell it apart from similar items. Put the most important words first.

- Start with the product type, then the fabric, then the key feature, then the colour.
- Keep titles under the marketplace limit; shorter titles are easier to scan on a phone.
- Use title case for product names and lower case for ordinary words in the middle of a title.
- Do not repeat a word in a title, and do not stuff it with search terms.
- Include the set size when a product comes as a set, for example "set of 3" or "3-piece".

## Bullets

Bullets carry the details that help a shopper decide. Each bullet should answer one question the
shopper is likely to have, in the order they are likely to ask it.

- The first bullet covers fabric and feel: what it is made of and how it feels to wear or touch.
- The second bullet covers fit and size: the cut, the length and how the sizes run.
- The third bullet covers design: the print, the embroidery, the neckline or the finish.
- The fourth bullet covers care: how to wash it and how to keep it looking new.
- The fifth bullet covers what is in the box: every piece, so there are no surprises.
- Start each bullet with a short label in capitals, followed by a plain sentence.

## Descriptions

The description is where we can tell a slightly longer story, but it should still be useful. Open
with the occasion the product suits, then describe how it looks and feels, and close with a short
line on care. Two or three short paragraphs are enough. Do not repeat the bullets word for word;
add detail instead, such as how the fabric drapes or how the colour pairs with other pieces.

## Home goods

For lamps, rugs and other home goods, lead with the material and the size, since shoppers need to
know whether the piece fits their space. Give dimensions in centimetres first and inches second.
Mention the finish and how to clean it. For lamps, state the bulb type and whether a bulb is
included. For rugs, state the pile height and whether the rug needs an underlay to stay in place.

## Regional language

Many of our customers read listings in more than one language. When we use a regional word for a
garment or a craft, we keep the common spelling and add a short plain description the first time
it appears. We never translate a craft name into a word that loses its meaning, and we do not mix
scripts inside a single word.

## Glossary

Use the preferred term on the right instead of the term on the left.

- kurta outfit: kurta set
- dupatta scarf: dupatta
- pure cotton blend: cotton blend
- free size: one size
- hand made: handmade
- table light: table lamp
- floor carpet: floor rug
- washable in machine: machine washable
- colour fast: colourfast
- best quality: premium cotton (or the exact fabric name)

## Review checklist

Before a listing goes to a reviewer, the writer reads it once more against this list.

- The title names the product type, fabric, key feature and colour, in that order.
- Every claim can be checked against the label, the product or a certificate.
- No word from the left side of the glossary appears anywhere in the copy.
- The bullets answer fabric, fit, design, care and contents, one each.
- The description adds detail rather than repeating the bullets.
- There are no promises about price, delivery or stock.
- The copy reads clearly aloud, at an even pace, with no shouting.

A reviewer who finds a problem sends the listing back with a short note naming the rule. The
writer fixes that part only, so the rest of the approved copy stays as it was.
